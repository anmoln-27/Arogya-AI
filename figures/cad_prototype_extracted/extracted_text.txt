P14: Title of the article “Arogya AI: Portable Multi-Modal Edge-AI Diagnostic Device for Rural Healthcare "     
P15: 
P16: Use of article 
P17: This article targets the needs of point-of-care health triaging and initial diagnosis in rural and resource-limited settings. Its purpose is to be utilized by front-line health care professionals (ASHA workers) to:
P18: •	Gather various physiological and biochemical measurements
P19: •	Facilitate health screening immediately after acquiring the data without the use of laboratories or internet connectivity
P20: •	Perform multi-modal health screening incorporating cardiopulmonary, thermal, spectral, and environmental sensing
P21: •	Facilitate objective decision-making at the point of care
P22: •	Do AI-based triage in the absence of internet connection using TinyML algorithms
P23: •	Apply the Deep Scan method for acquiring noise-free data
P24: •	Demonstrate real-time data acquired by displaying them on an OLED display and Red/Green LEDs
P25: •	Conduct tests for common diseases such as fever, hypoxia, arrhythmia, respiratory problems, and anaemia.
P26: 
P27: Brief Description of the design of the article:
P28: Purpose/use of the article:
P29: The suggested article involves a miniature diagnostic tool which contains several different types of sensors that are assembled in one compact enclosure, providing a full-scale health check in outdoor conditions. These include:
P30: Cardiopulmonary sensors (ECG, SpO₂/PPG, respiration)
P31: Fever sensor using thermal imaging
P32: Hemoglobin sensor based on spectral sensing
P33: Gas sensor and GSR sensor
P34: 
P35: These sensing systems have been incorporated into a single enclosure and are able to provide on-device inference powered by ESP32-S3 and machine learning models (TinyML). The proposed system provides for the following features:
P36: Single-user manipulation outside the clinical setting
P37: Organized access to sensors owing to their positioning
P38: Output in real-time using OLED and LEDs
P39: Offline functionality ensuring stability of the system operation in rural settings
P40: A 60-second “Deep Scan” sequence ensures optimal sensor contact.
P41: Novel aspect of the article sought for design protection (please provide a novelty statement of the article) 
P42: The uniqueness of the invention is found in the integrated configuration and diagnostics system architecture:
P43: 
P44: Configuration of a one-unit, multimodal diagnostic system based on the structural integration of multiple types of sensors (cardiac, respiratory, thermal, spectral, and environmental sensors) into a portable form factor, which is different from a traditional approach to a multi-device configuration.
P45: Internal system architecture, integrating I²C, SPI, and analog sensor interfaces via the embedded system, making possible spatial and functional integration of the system into the small housing enclosure.
P46: Unique design of user interface configuration, consisting of a small OLED display and a triage indicator in the form of a two-color LED light (Red/Green) to enable operation in a zero-literacy environment.
P47: Integrated physical design according to the workflow-driven rule of interactions between user and device (Idle > Deep scan > Results) ensuring additional stability in measurements and reduction of errors.
P48: Autonomous diagnostic embedded system, combining all the necessary parts of it (sensor interface, processor, display) inside the housing without using computing capabilities of other devices or the cloud.
P49: Physical device configuration and design including geometrical design, sensor arrangement and interface optimization for functioning in tough conditions
